# How to Get the Most Out of This Course

## The Most Important Rule

Do not just watch or read. Type every line of code yourself.

Reading about swimming does not teach you to swim. Programming is a skill, and skills are built through practice, not observation.

## Practical Guidelines

**Type the code, do not copy-paste.**
When you manually type code, your brain processes it differently. You notice syntax details, you make mistakes, and you learn from fixing those mistakes.

**Run the code frequently.**
After writing 2 to 3 lines, run it. Do not wait to write 50 lines and then run. Frequent execution helps you catch errors early and understand what each line does.

**Make intentional mistakes.**
Change a variable, delete a character, break the indentation — see what error you get. Understanding error messages is one of the most important skills in programming.

**Take breaks.**
Your brain needs time to process new information. A 10-minute break after 45 minutes of focused work will make you learn faster, not slower.

**Do not skip the practice tasks.**
The tasks at the end of each session exist for a reason. They push you to apply what you learned without following a step-by-step guide. This is how real-world programming works.

**Search when you are stuck — but try first.**
Before searching for a solution, spend at least 5 to 10 minutes attempting it on your own. Searching immediately removes the struggle, and the struggle is where learning happens.

**Use official documentation.**
Python has official documentation at docs.python.org. Learning to read documentation is a skill that separates average developers from good ones.

## Mindset

Errors are not failures. Every programmer — beginner and expert — encounters errors constantly. An error is the computer telling you exactly what it needs you to fix. Learn to read them carefully.

Progress feels slow at the start, then compounds quickly. The first week is always the hardest. Push through it.
